# No-Slop Writing

## Trigger

Use this skill for:

- Blog posts
- Engineering essays
- Technical notes
- Opinion pieces
- Draft rewrites
- Turning findings into readable writing

## Shape

Start with something that happened.

Use a concrete scene:

- A command passed
- A sheet stayed wrong
- A PR looked green
- A comment was marked stale without a link
- An artifact was deleted before proof existed

Then build:

1. What happened
2. What the writer expected
3. What broke
4. Why it wasted review time
5. What rule falls out of it

## Voice

Use proper capitalization.

Write in full paragraphs that read as connected prose.

Capitalize sentences normally, matching edited prose rather than the lowercase style used in agent chat.

Write like a review correction that found a story.

## Titles

The title should make the reader want the next paragraph.

Good:

- The CSV Passed. I Still Couldn't Review It.
- The PR Was Green. The Issue Was Still Wrong.
- The Artifact Was Gone Before the Proof Existed.

Bad:

- Review Surfaces Are Contracts
- The Importance of Validation
- Building Better Engineering Workflows

## Prefer

- State comparisons directly; skip the `Not X, but Y` template.
- Choose plain, specific words over `Real`, `Crucial`, `Pivotal`, `Key`, `Underscore`, `Landscape`.
- Open with the concrete scene, artifact, or failure; extract the principle after it.
- Vary sentence length across the paragraph so the rhythm reads as prose rather than one-line haiku beats.
- Let the ending land on the actual finding, stated plainly.
- Present the one real trade-off in play, skipping manufactured "both sides" framing.
- Size the language to the actual stakes of the finding.

## Check

Before final output:

- Does the first paragraph contain a scene?
- Is there a mismatch?
- Can the reader see the consequence?
- Does the principle come after the story?
- Does the ending land without sounding like a slogan?
